[TrueLayer](https://truelayer.com) is a UK Open Banking provider. Its Data API reads current accounts, savings accounts, and credit cards from most UK banks and card issuers. You bring your own TrueLayer app, so Sure connects using your credentials directly.

See [Transaction history and import limits](/providers/transaction-history) for initial sync defaults, history limits, and options for older transactions.

## How it works

TrueLayer uses OAuth 2.0 to connect your bank. Sure never sees your banking credentials — authentication happens directly through TrueLayer and your bank.

1. Create a TrueLayer app and save its credentials in Sure
2. Click **Connect a bank** to open TrueLayer's consent dialog
3. Choose your bank and approve access through your bank's app or website
4. Sure imports your accounts and begins syncing balances and transactions

Each bank connection has its own consent. You can connect multiple banks by clicking **Connect a bank** again for each one.

## Prerequisites

- A TrueLayer account and app from [console.truelayer.com](https://console.truelayer.com)
- Your TrueLayer **client ID** and **client secret**
- Sure's callback URL registered in your TrueLayer app

## Setting up TrueLayer

### Create a TrueLayer app

1. Sign up at [console.truelayer.com](https://console.truelayer.com) and create an app
2. Copy the **client ID** and **client secret**
3. Under **Allowed redirect URIs**, add the callback URL shown in Sure's TrueLayer panel — it is `https://<your-sure-host>/truelayer_items/callback` and must match exactly

TrueLayer accepts plain `http` only for `localhost`. A public instance requires HTTPS.

A client ID starting with `sandbox-` switches Sure to TrueLayer's sandbox, which provides a **Mock Bank** for testing without a real account.

### Connect a bank in Sure

1. In Sure, go to **Settings > Providers** and open the **TrueLayer** panel
2. Enter your client ID and client secret, then click **Save**
3. Click **Connect a bank** — Sure redirects you to TrueLayer's consent dialog
4. Select your bank and approve access
5. After the callback, Sure imports your accounts and starts syncing

### Set up imported accounts

After the first import, Sure shows the accounts returned by your bank. Go to the **Accounts** page and run **Set Up New Accounts** to choose an account type for each one (depository, credit card, or loan), or skip accounts you do not want to track. You can also link a TrueLayer account to an existing manual account from that account's page.

Set up accounts within a few minutes of approving consent — UK banks release more than 90 days of history only during that window.

## Transaction history and syncing

### Initial backfill

Right after you connect a bank, Sure fetches transaction history going back in one-year chunks up to `TRUELAYER_BACKFILL_YEARS` (default 7 years, range 1–20). The bank releases extended history only for a few minutes after you approve consent, so this backfill runs immediately at first sync.

If the initial import runs after that window closes, the connection card shows a **Reconnect** button. Reconnecting opens a new consent and a new history window.

The account setup dialog has an **Import transactions from** date. Leave it blank to import everything the bank provided, or set a date to exclude older transactions. Subsequent syncs and backfills also respect this date.

### Ongoing syncs

After the backfill, regular syncs fetch transactions from a week before the last sync and never more than 89 days back, which keeps requests within UK banks' unattended-access limits. Rate-limited requests are not retried — UK Open Banking often allows only a few requests per endpoint per day for unattended access.

Transactions are matched by TrueLayer's stable transaction ID, so re-syncing never creates duplicates. Pending transactions are not imported; they appear once they are booked. Card charges are imported as spending and refunds as income.

### Consent expiry

UK Open Banking consent lasts 90 days. Sure displays the expiry date in the TrueLayer panel and shows a **Reconnect** button starting two weeks before expiry, or immediately if your bank revokes access. Reconnecting reuses the same TrueLayer account IDs, so linked accounts keep their full history.

## Supported account types

- **Current accounts** — depository accounts
- **Savings accounts** — depository savings accounts
- **Credit cards** — credit card accounts

## Configuration

| Variable | Default | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TRUELAYER_REDIRECT_URI` | derived from request | Callback URL sent to TrueLayer. Set this when a proxy does not forward the public host and scheme. |
| `TRUELAYER_BACKFILL_YEARS` | `7` | Years of history fetched right after a bank is connected. Accepts values from 1 to 20. |

## Troubleshooting

### History is missing after the first sync

If fewer than 90 days of history imported, the initial sync likely ran after your bank's consent window closed. The connection card offers a **Reconnect** button in this case. Reconnecting opens a new consent, giving Sure another window to fetch the full history.

To avoid this, run account setup and let the first sync complete within a few minutes of approving consent.

### The bank rejected authorization

If the callback returns an error, the authorization failed at TrueLayer or your bank. Common causes:

- The redirect URI in Sure does not match the one registered in your TrueLayer app exactly — check for trailing slashes or HTTP vs HTTPS mismatches
- The consent was completed in a different browser or tab than the one where you started it — start the flow again in the same browser
- The refresh token has expired — click **Reconnect** to start a new consent

### Connection shows "requires update"

The bank revoked access or the refresh token expired. Click **Reconnect** in the TrueLayer panel to authorize again. Sure reuses the existing TrueLayer account IDs, so your linked accounts and history are preserved.

### Sandbox mode

A client ID starting with `sandbox-` connects Sure to TrueLayer's sandbox environment, which provides a **Mock Bank** for testing. Use a production client ID (without the `sandbox-` prefix) to connect real bank accounts.
