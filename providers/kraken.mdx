Kraken is a cryptocurrency exchange. Sure connects to Kraken using a read-only API key and imports your combined crypto holdings and spot trade history.

See [Transaction history and import limits](/providers/transaction-history) for initial sync defaults, history limits, and options for older transactions.

## What is imported

Sure imports one combined Crypto exchange account per Kraken connection, containing:

- Current crypto holdings and balances
- Spot trade fills (buy/sell history)
- Deposits and withdrawals — each imported as two entries: the principal and a separate fee entry, so the transfer can be matched to your receiving account
- Staking rewards and Earn income
- Dust sweeps ("convert small balances") as paired Sweep In / Sweep Out entries
- Standalone fiat fees

Crypto ledger entries (deposits, withdrawals, staking rewards, and so on) are recorded as trades that carry a unit quantity and the price on the day they moved, not as cash transactions. This keeps your crypto position history accurate and separate from your cash balance.

For spot trades, Sure reads the filled quantity and cash movement from the Kraken ledger rather than from TradesHistory. The ledger nets each side of a trade against its fee — including fees charged in the base asset, which TradesHistory does not expose — so the recorded quantity and cash reflect what actually moved. If ledger rows are not available for a trade (for example, before the "Query Ledger Entries" permission was granted), Sure falls back to TradesHistory values and corrects the entry automatically once the ledger rows arrive.

## Setting up Kraken

### Step 1: Create a Kraken API key

1. Go to [Kraken API settings](https://pro.kraken.com/app/settings/api).
2. Create a new API key with the following permissions only:
   - **Query Funds**
   - **Query Closed Orders & Trades**
   - **Query Ledger Entries**

<Warning>
Do not grant trading, cancellation, withdrawal, export, or transfer permissions. Sure only needs read access. Granting write permissions is unnecessary and a security risk.
</Warning>

3. Copy the **API Key** and **Private Key** shown after creation.

### Step 2: Add the connection in Sure

1. Go to **Settings → Providers**.
2. Find the **Kraken** section.
3. Enter a connection name, paste your **API Key** and **Private Key**, and save.
4. Sure begins syncing your Kraken exchange account automatically.

### Step 3: Import the account

After the first sync, your Kraken exchange account appears in the setup queue. Click **Import Account** in the provider panel to add it to your account list.

## Multiple connections

If you have multiple Kraken accounts, add a separate named connection for each one. Each connection imports one combined crypto account.

## Troubleshooting

### Credentials need updating

If the connection shows a reconnect prompt, your API key may have been revoked or the credentials may have expired. Generate a new API key from Kraken and update the connection in **Settings → Providers → Kraken**.

### No accounts found

After adding a connection, wait for the first sync to complete. If no accounts appear after syncing, verify that your API key has the **Query Funds** permission enabled.

### Balance shown as approximate

When the exact exchange rate for a given date is unavailable, Sure shows the balance as approximate. The balance updates to the exact figure on the next sync when rate data becomes available.

### Crypto holdings history looks wrong for older accounts

If your Kraken account was connected before October 2026, crypto ledger entries may have been recorded as cash transactions rather than unit-carrying trades. Sure corrects these automatically on the next sync — no re-import or manual action is needed. Entries you have manually edited or matched into a transfer are left as-is.

### Trade quantities or cash amounts look slightly off

Kraken can charge a trade's fee in either the base asset (for example, BTC on a BTC/USD buy) or the quote currency. When the fee comes out of the base asset, TradesHistory reports the gross quantity with no indication that units were taken as a fee.

Sure now reads trade quantity and cash from the Kraken ledger, which nets fees correctly on both sides. Trades imported before the "Query Ledger Entries" API permission was active — or before the ledger backfill reached them — held the uncorrected gross figures. Sure corrects those trades automatically on the next sync once the ledger rows are available. Trades you have manually edited or locked are left unchanged.
