---
title: "Goals"
description: "Track savings goals and reserves in Sure"
---

Goals let you earmark money in your depository or investment accounts toward a specific purpose. Each goal links to one or more accounts and tracks progress against a target amount.

## Goal types

When you create a goal you choose its kind. The kind determines what 100% means and what actions are available.

### One-off goal

A one-off goal has a finish line. Once you reach the target amount, the goal is complete and its earmark is released. Use one-off goals for anything with a definite end: a vacation fund, a new laptop, a house down payment.

### Reserve to maintain

A reserve has no finish line — it is a level to hold, not an achievement to close. Use a reserve for an emergency fund, a buffer account, or any amount you want to keep in place.

A reserve cannot be marked complete. Completing a goal releases the money it was earmarking; for a reserve, releasing that money is the opposite of the goal's purpose. To close a reserve, archive or delete it instead.

## Status

### One-off goal statuses

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| On track | You have a target date and your current pace meets the required monthly amount |
| Behind | You have a target date but your current pace falls short |
| No target date | The goal has no deadline — no pace calculation applies |
| Reached | The balance meets or exceeds the target |

### Reserve statuses

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Funded | The balance meets the target — the reserve is whole |
| Depleted | The balance is below the target — the reserve has a shortfall |

A depleted reserve sorts to the top of the goals list alongside behind-pace one-off goals, because both need attention. A funded reserve sorts near the bottom — it is working as intended.

## Display order

The goals index sorts active goals in this order:

1. **Behind / Depleted** — goals that need attention
2. **On track** — goals progressing toward their deadline
3. **No target date** — open-ended one-off goals
4. **Funded** — reserves that are whole
5. **Paused** — goals you have stopped feeding

Within each group, goals sort alphabetically.

## Pace

Pace is the rolling 90-day net inflow into a goal's linked accounts, divided by three, expressed as a monthly figure.

Pace applies only to one-off goals with a target date. Reserves have no deadline and therefore no monthly target to compute pace against. The "save X per month to catch up" prompt is suppressed for reserves.

## Completing a goal

The complete action is available only for one-off goals. It releases the goal's earmarked money back to the account, marks the goal closed, and moves it to the completed list.

If you want to stop tracking a reserve, use **Archive** (keeps it as a record) or **Delete** (removes it entirely). Both options are in the kebab menu on the goal's page.

## Pausing a goal

Pausing stops the pace clock. A paused goal keeps its earmark — the money stays reserved — but it no longer contributes to "behind pace" counts or catch-up prompts.

## Earmarking and shared accounts

A goal earmarks the balance of every account linked to it. If two goals share the same account, both read the full balance and both count it toward their progress. This is a known limitation; the balance is not split between goals.

## Plan hub

The Plan hub shows a summary of your active goals. A reserve that is depleted shows a warning indicator there, the same as a one-off goal that is behind pace. A funded reserve does not trigger a warning.
