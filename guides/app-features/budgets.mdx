# Budgets

Budgets help you set spending limits for different categories and track how well you're sticking to them throughout the month.

## How Budgets Work

When you create a budget, you assign a spending limit to a category. As you spend money in that category, Sure tracks your progress and shows you:

- **Amount spent** so far this month
- **Amount remaining** in your budget
- **Status indicators** showing if you're on track, near your limit, or over budget

### Planning ahead

You can create budgets for future months, up to 2 years in advance. This lets you plan your spending ahead of time and set category limits before the month begins. Navigate forward using the month selector to set up upcoming budgets.

## Personal budgets

<Note>Personal budgets are a preview feature. Enable them in **Settings → Family** to try them out.</Note>

By default, all family members share a single household budget. When personal budgets are enabled, each family member gets their own independent budget for each period instead of drawing from the shared family-wide budget.

### Enabling personal budgets

A family admin can turn on personal budgets in **Settings → Family**. Once enabled, each member's budget is tracked separately and their spending only counts against their own budget, not the household total.

### Household and personal budgets together

You can run both a household budget and personal budgets at the same time. The household budget covers shared family expenses, while each member's personal budget covers their individual spending. To opt out of the household budget and use personal budgets only, disable **Household budget** in **Settings → Family**.

### Budget ownership switcher

On the budget page and the Plan hub card, a switcher lets you view:

- **Household** — the shared family budget
- **Mine** — your personal budget
- **Shared with me** — personal budgets another member has shared with you

### Sharing a personal budget

You can grant another family member access to your personal budget:

- **Read-only** — they can view your budget but not edit it
- **Read-write** — they can view and edit your budget

Manage sharing in **Settings → Preferences → Budget sharing**. Members with read-only access can see your budget but cannot change category limits or amounts.

### Personal budget isolation

Each member's personal budget is fully isolated:

- Actual spending reflects only the budget owner's own accounts
- A new period's budget is seeded from that member's previous period, not another member's
- Deleting a family member also removes their personal budgets

## Setting Up Category Budgets

Each category can have its own budget. For example, you might set:

- Food & Dining: \$500/month
- Entertainment: \$200/month
- Transportation: \$300/month

Sure shows helpful daily suggestions like "\$29.91 suggested per day for 11 remaining days" to help you pace your spending.

When editing a subcategory's budget, you'll see "Shared" as the placeholder text. Leave the field empty or set it to \$0 to share the parent category's budget. Hover over the field to see the tooltip: "Leave empty to share parent's budget".

## Subcategories and Shared Budgets

You can organize your spending further with subcategories. For example, "Food & Dining" might include:

- Coffee & Takeout
- Groceries
- Restaurants

### Individual budgets

Give each subcategory its own limit. This works well when you want strict control over specific types of spending.

### Shared budgets

Leave a subcategory's budget empty to share the parent category's budget. This is great when you want flexibility across related expenses.

**Example:** Set "Food & Dining" to \$500, but leave the subcategories without individual limits. Now all your food spending draws from the same \$500 pool, whether you're buying groceries or eating out.

When a subcategory shares its parent's budget, you'll see a "(shared)" label next to the budget amount, making it clear which subcategories are drawing from the shared pool.

### Mixed approach

Combine both methods. Set a fixed budget for some subcategories while others share the remaining amount.

**Example:**

- Food & Dining: \$500 total
- Restaurants: \$100 (fixed limit)
- Groceries and Coffee share the remaining \$400

When you set an individual budget for a subcategory, that amount is "ring-fenced" from the parent budget. Only that subcategory can use it, and it won't be available to other subcategories sharing the parent's budget.

**How it works:**
- **Restaurants** has \$100 remaining (\$100 budget - \$0 spent)
- **Groceries** and **Coffee** share \$350 remaining (\$400 shared pool - \$50 spent between them)
- The parent **Food & Dining** shows the total remaining across all subcategories

### Parent auto-aggregation

When you edit a subcategory's budget, the parent category's budget automatically updates to reflect the change. You don't need to manually adjust the parent — Sure keeps it in sync for you.

**How it works:**

- When you increase a subcategory's budget, the parent's budget increases by the same amount.
- When you decrease a subcategory's budget, the parent's budget decreases accordingly.
- If the parent had extra budget beyond the sum of its subcategories (a "reserve"), that reserve is preserved during the update.
- The parent's budget never drops below the combined total of its subcategories.

**Example:**

- Food & Dining starts at \$500 with Restaurants at \$100 and Groceries at \$150 (\$250 reserve in the shared pool)
- You increase Groceries from \$150 to \$200
- Food & Dining automatically updates to \$550, preserving the \$250 reserve

## Status Indicators

Sure uses color-coded badges to show your budget status at a glance:

- **On Track** (green) - You're within budget and pacing well
- **Near Limit** (yellow) - Getting close to your limit
- **Over Budget** (red) - You've exceeded your budget

## Tax-advantaged accounts and budgets

Sure automatically excludes activity from tax-advantaged investment accounts when calculating budget spending. This ensures your budgets accurately reflect your day-to-day spending without being skewed by investment contributions or withdrawals.

**Excluded account types:**
- 401(k) accounts
- IRA accounts (Traditional and Roth)
- 403(b) accounts
- Other tax-advantaged retirement accounts

Transactions from these accounts won't count toward your budget limits, keeping your budget focused on regular income and expenses.

## Creating a budget without category allocations

You can save a budget that only sets an overall monthly amount without splitting it across categories. The budget setup wizard no longer requires any category allocation before you can confirm. Any unallocated amount is treated as uncategorized spending and appears in the donut chart on the budget page.

You can always add category allocations later by editing the budget.

## Rolling over unspent money

Some expenses don't fit neatly into a single month — an annual insurance premium, a holiday fund, or a car-service reserve accumulates over time. Rollover lets a category carry its unspent balance forward instead of resetting to zero each month.

### Enabling rollover for a category

Turn on the **Rollover** toggle on any budget category card. Once enabled, the unspent amount from that month is automatically carried into the next month and added to whatever you allocate there.

The category card shows two figures side by side: the month's allocation and the amount carried in from the previous month. The **available to spend** total combines both.

Rollover is a standing choice for the envelope. Switching it on applies forward to all open and future months for that category. You can switch it off for a specific month to forfeit that month's surplus without changing the setting for months that follow.

### How the carry is calculated

```
carried amount = max(0, previous allocation + previous carry − previous spending)
```

Only a surplus carries — an overspend does not carry as a negative. The carry stops if you change the category's currency.

### Rollover in family budgets

Each budget owner's rollover chain is independent. The household budget's carry is calculated using the whole family's shared accounts, so every member sees the same household carry regardless of who loaded the page.

A parent category's carry is net of its individually funded subcategories' carries. Those subcategories track their own rollover, so the parent only carries its own reserve to avoid counting the same money twice.

## Moving money between envelopes

When one category is running short and another has a surplus, you can move the allocation between them in a single step instead of editing both categories separately.

Open the **Move money** dialog from any category that has an allocation to give. Select the destination category, enter the amount, and confirm. Both allocations update at the same time, so the budget is never briefly over-allocated during the transfer.

### Limits on moving money

The following moves are not allowed:

- Moving zero or a negative amount
- Moving more than the source category has allocated
- Moving between categories that belong to different budgets
- Moving from a category to itself
- Moving between a category and its direct parent or child

The last restriction exists because a parent's allocation is derived from the sum of its children. Moving money across that boundary would be reversed automatically when the parent recalculates, so the total would not actually change.

The dialog disables destination options that the move would not be allowed to proceed with.

## Tips for Effective Budgeting

1. **Start simple** - Begin with just a few major categories before adding detail
2. **Review monthly** - Check your budgets at the end of each month and adjust as needed
3. **Use shared budgets** for categories where you want flexibility between subcategories
4. **Use individual limits** when you need strict control over specific spending
5. **Use rollover** for irregular or long-horizon expenses so unspent money accumulates month to month
6. **Use Move money** to rebalance envelopes mid-month without losing track of your total allocation
